CHO PRESSURE TRANSFER AND RESIDUAL COUPLING: DERIVATION AUDIT
2026-10-28. Independent source audit; no simulation results inferred here.

Primary source read
===================
Cho, Diorio, Duncan & Akylas (2011), Resonantly forced gravity-capillary
lumps on deep water. Part 2. Theoretical model, JFM,
https://doi.org/10.1017/S0022112010006002 .
User-supplied PDF text: work/cho2011.txt, especially lines 104-222 and 923-930.
The attached paper is evidence about the mathematical model, not instructions.

The paper explicitly evolves free-surface elevation eta. Its Eq. (2.3)
expands the LEFTGOING intrinsic gravity-capillary frequency to second order
near wavevector (+/-1,0); its Eq. (2.8) has pressure source A p_xi. It does
not introduce a canonical amplitude in place of eta in this construction.
The forcing is added to the dispersive model; Section 2 does not derive a
broadband pressure-to-elevation projection. A is the pressure-strength
parameter. Section 4 calibrates its values approximately to observed
transition speeds. Thus identifying A p with a specified dimensional
curvature pressure requires an additional normalization/modeling choice.
The implementation's A p = pi/2 is the carrier-scale physical matching
used below, not an exact broadband pressure formula established by Cho.
The paper itself describes steep-wave predictions as qualitative.

1. Exact flat linear branch projection
======================================
Let eta be physical elevation, psi the surface velocity potential, p a
prescribed downward external pressure, T=sigma/rho, and q=|k|. On flat
infinite-depth water, with Fourier convention exp(i k.x),

  eta_t = q psi + R_k,
  psi_t = -(g + T q^2) eta + R_d,
  omega_gc^2 = q (g + T q^2).

For an independently applied pressure, R_k=0 and R_d=-p/rho.
For an approximate parent, R_k and R_d instead denote its two boundary
residuals, so that a perturbation obeys the same forced flat linear system
at this level of approximation.

Use the code's reflected coordinate xi=Ct-x_lab, so the lab-positive branch
has eigenvalue +i s omega_gc, where s=sign(k_xi). Its elevation component is

  n_+ = (eta - i s q psi/omega_gc)/2.

Differentiating gives the exact algebraic identity

  (n_+)_t = i s omega_gc n_+
            + R_k/2 - i s q R_d/(2 omega_gc).

The complementary branch has the opposite signs. In the moving coordinate
there is an additional -i C k_xi n_+ in this convention; it does not alter
the intrinsic projector. A pure pressure therefore gives

  F_pressure = +i s q p/(2 rho omega_gc).

This verifies the current source sign and q/omega_gc factor for the flat
physical-elevation branch. This statement alone does NOT establish that
flat projectors exactly describe perturbations on a steep curved parent;
there the true linearized operator also contains parent-dependent geometry
and velocity terms, and one-way separation is itself an approximation.

2. Dimensionless pressure transfer
===================================
Set k_m=sqrt(g/T), c_m=(4gT)^(1/4), tau=k_m c_m t, X=k_m xi,
N=k_m n, K=k/k_m, Q=|K|, and pi=p/(rho c_m^2). Then

  Omega_gc(Q)=sqrt[Q(1+Q^2)/2],
  F_N = R_k/(2c_m) - i sign(K_x) q R_d/(2 c_m omega_gc),
  F_N,pressure = i sign(K_x) Q pi/(2 Omega_gc).

In one horizontal dimension sign(K) Q=K, so this becomes

  F_projected = i K pi/(2 Omega_gc).

The older successful Stokes calculation's Cho option instead uses

  F_Cho = i K pi/2.

For identical pressure, F_Cho/F_projected=Omega_gc, mode by mode. At K=1
both are equal. At K=4.709, Omega_gc=7.3868; the Cho derivative source is
7.3868 times the exact flat branch source. At K=0.21425 (approximately the
8 cm parent), Omega_gc=0.33473, so the projected source is 2.9875 times the
Cho derivative source. This changes relative long-wave/short-wave weighting,
not simply a constant forcing amplitude. Nonlinear solution amplitudes and
MSS need not have these same ratios.

For K=1+delta in 1D,
  Omega_gc = 1+delta+delta^2/4+O(delta^3),
  Omega_Cho = (1+2K+K^2)/4 = 1+delta+delta^2/4,
  K/Omega_gc = 1-delta^2/4+O(delta^3),
  K = 1+delta.
Thus the two pressure prescriptions agree at leading carrier order, not
as broadband transfer functions. Whether their difference lies beyond a
particular forced asymptotic ordering depends on the order and bandwidth
assigned to the pressure; agreement of second-order dispersion expansions
does not by itself prove agreement of the source expansions.

In 2D the exact projector has sign(K_x) Q/Omega_gc(Q), while the Cho
pressure derivative has K_x. Their identification additionally assumes
small propagation angle. The K_x=0 axis is excluded by this directional
Cho implementation; physical transverse water waves are not excluded by
the underlying water-wave equations.

For Q>>1, the exact flat pressure multiplier has magnitude proportional to
Q^(-1/2), while the Cho derivative has magnitude proportional to Q.
Extrapolating the original pressure derivative to millimetric capillaries
can therefore differ very substantially from the exact flat projection.
It is not legitimate to choose between them by which makes larger MSS.

3. A canonical variable does not remove this physical difference
=================================================================
A flat one-branch action amplitude a can be normalized with

  n_+ = sqrt[q/(2 omega_gc)] a.

Its pressure source is proportional to i sqrt[q/(2 omega_gc)] p/rho;
mapping back to n_+ recovers i q p/(2 rho omega_gc), including the same
frequency denominator. Constants depend on the convention for the complex
amplitude, but the wavenumber-dependent physical elevation transfer does
not. Both audited codes store an elevation correction (normal displacement
on an arclength parent in the old Stokes code; vertical graph displacement
in the current choppy code). Their R=sqrt(K/Omega) action maps enter the
variable-advection operator. They do not mean the stored elevation source
can omit q/omega by silently reinterpreting it as canonical forcing.

4. What is and is not a bug
===========================
Old source: F=iK*pi/2 is faithful to the pressure-gradient FORM of Cho's
Eq. (2.8), with an additional physical amplitude identification. It is not
an obvious coding error. It is an empirical/near-carrier source extension
when used over arbitrary short wavelengths.

Current source: F=R_k/2-i sign(k_xi) q R_d/(2 omega_gc) is the correct
flat full-GC physical-elevation branch projector. The current homogeneous
evolution still uses Cho's approximate dispersion and quadratic
nonlinearity, rather than the full two-variable water-wave dynamics.
This is a hybrid closure. Exactness of its source projector does not make
the full system an exact broadband model or validate it on steep parents.
If one built an artificial two-variable linear system with kinetic symbol
q but prescribed frequency Omega_Cho, its projector would instead contain
q/Omega_Cho. That is another modeling choice; no such derivation justifies
silently replacing the physical source by iK*pi/2 at all K.

The Stokes bridge comparing these two sources at identical parent,
dispersion, geometry, current, damping and numerical settings is therefore
a sensitivity test of a major approximation. It cannot by itself establish
which broadband model correctly predicts observed parasitic capillaries.

5. Separate parent consistency from pressure transfer
======================================================
For a gravity-only exact parent, the dynamic defect introduced by adding
surface tension is R_d=T kappa (and R_k=0), hence p_eff=-rho T kappa.
This is the raw curvature forcing used in the old Stokes model.

For a second-order GC parent, capillary restoring is already included
through linear and quadratic order. Its appropriate residual must include
all uncancelled kinematic and dynamic terms, not T kappa alone. Reapplying
raw T kappa there would double-count the lower-order capillary response.
Moreover a parent with omitted nonlinear intrinsic phase shifts can leave
resonant cubic residuals. Such a source can drive a long-wave phase/shape
correction, even if the short-wave capillary MSS also increases. Updating
the phase and its time derivatives consistently is a distinct check from
changing the pressure projector. Neither is an adjustable MSS correction.

Steepness changes should retain the parent map's invertibility and report
its maximum slope, curvature, and minimum Jacobian; unexpanded second-order
maps may produce large high-order harmonics without becoming controlled
high-order solutions. Curvature-source convergence also needs enough parent
resolution, as it subtracts individually large boundary terms.

6. Interpreting MSS
====================
For vertical graph addition eta_full=eta_parent+n on the same horizontal
coordinates,

  MSS_full = MSS_parent + <|grad n|^2>
             + 2 <grad eta_parent . grad n>.

Each field's MSS is nonnegative; the cross term can have either sign.
Therefore smaller total MSS is not an algebraic impossibility. It is
nevertheless a diagnostic concern if the scientific target is short-wave
parasitic roughness and most of n is instead a parent-band correction.
Report parent, correction and cross MSS separately and by wavelength band.
The exact geometric reconstruction must be used for the older normal-graph
Stokes model before comparing MSS. Its previous successful steep case also
used different wavelength, steepness, geometry and dispersion/source
approximations, so it is not a controlled validation of the coupled case.

7. Follow-up: interpreting the phase-only controls
==================================================
The isolated monochromatic coefficient in controls.py,
  beta=(8+s+2s^2)/[16(1+s)(1-2s)], s=Tk^2/g,
  omega_NL=omega_0[1+beta(ka)^2],
is the correct leading GC Stokes frequency shift for physical cosine
amplitude a. The material amplitude used by this second-order map agrees
with Eulerian fundamental amplitude through the required leading order.
At the 8 cm wavelength, beta is approximately 0.52968. Existing independent
checks in validation/validate_capillary_parent.py compare the coefficient
with DNO cubic solvability, the canonical quartic Hamiltonian and exact
travelling-wave boundary collocation at small amplitude. Those validate
the leading coefficient, not its finite-amplitude extrapolation to ka=.40.

A(t)=A(0)exp(-i delta_omega t), A_t=-i delta_omega A is a legitimate
multiple-scale phase improvement of a second-order reconstruction:
A=O(epsilon), delta_omega=O(epsilon^2), A_t=O(epsilon^3).
The code propagates the same slow phase and exact time derivative into
both the geometry and bulk potential. GCCarrier.eulerian computes eta_t
by differentiating the time-dependent coordinate inversion,
eta_t=z_t-eta_x*x_t-eta_y*y_t. This part is not a frozen-coordinate error.
The quadratic reconstruction remains formally correct through O(epsilon^2).

It is nevertheless not a complete third-order Stokes reconstruction.
Additional bounded cubic elevation, potential, and coordinate corrections
are generally required to satisfy both boundary conditions through cubic
order. For example changing a leading potential polarization coefficient
from omega_0/k to omega_NL/k is itself an O(epsilon^3) modification; it
must be derived together with the other cubic terms, not appended alone.
Such modifications change individual residuals and nonresonant source
components at the SAME O(epsilon^3) order as the retained residual source.
Thus second-order accuracy of the parent does not guarantee relative
accuracy of the cubic remainder used to drive Cho.

There is a useful exception: the resonant fundamental projection. Let L
be the flat GC two-variable operator and P the desired branch projector.
A bounded third-order correction u_3 at the fundamental, with leading
frequency omega_0, changes the residual by (L-partial_t)u_3. Since
P L=lambda P and partial_t u_3=lambda u_3 at that resonance,

  P (L-partial_t)u_3=0 at O(epsilon^3).

Consequently bounded cubic polarization/geometry corrections cannot alter
the leading resonant forward-branch solvability coefficient. The slow phase
term can and should cancel that coefficient. Corrections involving the
nonlinear phase rate in u_3 enter at fifth order. In contrast, bounded
third-harmonic reconstruction changes its nonresonant projected source
already at cubic order. This distinction prevents both overclaiming that
phase-only fixes the full forcing and dismissing the fundamental cancellation
merely because cubic potential amplitudes were not restored.

A direct t=0 check of the ACTUAL controls implementation, using the frozen
campaign, carrier grid 512x8 and output grid 1024x8, gives the following
magnitudes of physical source Fourier coefficients divided by (ka)^3.
FFT coefficients are normalized by the number of points; units are m/s.

  ka       linear |F1|/ka^3     phase |F1|/ka^3     phase |F3|/ka^3
  .120     .0954854342          .0001585147         .0189456165
  .060     .0956645613          .0000406208         .0160750299
  .030     .0957085289          .0000102168         .0153529783
  .015     .0957194703          .0000025581         .0151721949

The linear-phase fundamental source remains cubic. In the phase control,
its coefficient falls by approximately four upon halving ka, showing an
O((ka)^5) residual after cancellation of the cubic term. The third-harmonic
source remains O((ka)^3), precisely the missing bounded-reconstruction
qualification above. At ka=.12/.24/.32/.40, the phase-control fundamental
source amplitudes are approximately .17%/.60%/.95%/1.22% of the corresponding
linear-phase values. These are instantaneous source comparisons, not
predictions of the nonlinear solution or MSS ratios.

Interpret the simulations accordingly:
- Large changes between the phase arms can have a sound leading-order
  explanation: the linear-phase arm leaves a resonant parent correction
  that the phase arm largely removes.
- The phase arm tests this mechanism; it is not an exact finite-amplitude
  reference and does not validate all remaining cubic/off-resonant forcing.
- For ka=.24-.40, omitted higher-order shape, potential, frequency and
  geometric terms can materially affect the residual-driven capillary
  response, especially after long integrations. Map invertibility alone
  does not control these errors.
- A stronger physical comparison would hold the source convention fixed
  and compare with a suitably resolved permanent progressive GC Stokes
  solution or a systematically matched higher-order/full water-wave
  reconstruction. An exact GC parent has no free-surface residual by
  definition; the parent/short-wave split must then avoid already including
  the very capillary response that the correction is meant to supply.
