The deliberately small addition

Two coefficients.
An evolving parent wave.

The prognostic parent retains complex amplitudes, phases, gravity–capillary dispersion, and two-way coupling to the three-dimensional current. Wind and damping enter as extra amplitude tendencies. The fixed and observed controls use prescribed amplitudes and linear phases.

This first 3 cm test uses a narrow nine-mode parent band, linear intrinsic evolution, and carrier-self surface reconstruction. Broad-band cubic interactions are excluded because of the near-resonant 3 cm regime. The proposed parent loss remains an empirical surrogate.

Γ denotes an energy growth or loss rate. Amplitude tendencies contain the factor ½. The steepness uses the whole designated parent band, normalized to ak for a sinusoid. The common factor ℓ ≤ 1 limits wave-input momentum to the available stress; it is not fitted.

Calibration, momentum accounting, and interpretation

Wave observations set the coefficients

The initial coefficient estimates come from wave evolution, not dye-depth fitting. The 3 cm measured series is total characteristic along-wind slope; interpreting it as a single parent is a reduced-model assumption. Transferring its loss coefficient to another wavelength is a sensitivity experiment.

Stress is shared consistently

The source-induced wave momentum change receives an equal and opposite horizontal-mean Eulerian-current tendency at each depth. Wind stress is imposed once, and the wave-input limiter enforces its available budget. The analysis checks the combined momentum change against the applied wind impulse.

Cho is a separate field

The four main cases retain a one-way Cho calculation with the existing 2.4× molecular disturbance damping; the two refined cases focus on the parent, current, and dye. The new coefficient acts on the parent. The computed Cho response does not supply a reciprocal parent-energy debit or feed directly into the dye.

What constitutes a useful result

A plausible wave history and dye response with fixed coefficients is useful evidence. Software checks, agreement between numerical controls, and fitting the observed wave curve do not by themselves establish physical validity or spatial convergence.
