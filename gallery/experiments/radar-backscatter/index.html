Backscatter, Doppler, and apparent current

Backscatter measures returned power. The Doppler centroid measures the mean frequency shift; the Doppler width describes its spread. Apparent-current differences express a frequency shift as an equivalent horizontal speed along the radar look.

How to interpret this first experiment

  • This is a preliminary coherent first-order small-slope approximation (SSA1), with a linear Bragg reference. It is not the full weighted-curvature calculation in Nouguier et al. (2011).
  • Microwave phase is retained. These results are not optical brightness from the blue surface movies, and no backward ray-tracing or shadowing correction is implied.
  • The finite simulated patch is 0.32 × 0.64 m. Its response is a controlled surface experiment, not a calibrated kilometre-scale scatterometer footprint.
  • The strongest Cho events have steep local slopes. Passing numerical checks does not establish the accuracy of this electromagnetic approximation for those events.
  • For SSA1 at a fixed look, polarization changes a geometry-independent coefficient. Normalized Doppler centroid and width therefore coincide for HH and VV in this approximation; a stronger polarization-dependent kinematic model requires additional scattering terms.
  • An apparent-current difference is a diagnostic of retrieval ambiguity. It is not a new mean current, and no wind-retrieval product is computed here.

    Reference: Nouguier, Guérin & Soriano (2011), nonlinear ocean Doppler signatures.
